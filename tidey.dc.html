01The Problem

Every alert starts with groundwork

Before anyone can decide, someone has to piece the story together: who the customer is, what’s normal for them, what changed and when.

10–15min typically allowed for one retail fraud alert
6–8 systems checked in a single review
30–40% of retail alerts need a call to the customer

From interviews with fraud teams in North America and Europe (NICE Actimize, 2026). NICE Actimize sells fraud software, so these are vendor figures.

The bet

An agent that knows when not to decide

A person approves every block.

04Design Decisions

How far Tidey is trusted

Six choices shaped how it decides and where a person steps in.

Questions, not one decision

Probabilities make every line tunable

Jev answers narrow questions. Code combines the answers, so each threshold can be read, tested and moved on its own.

Rules in code, not prompts

Policy stays policy

Moving where Tidey closes an alert is a one-line change a fraud lead can review, not a prompt rewrite.

Recommend, don’t act

A person approves every block

Freezing an account hits a real customer and is hard to undo. Tidey recommends and explains; an analyst decides.

A hard cap

Never closed alone at $5,000 or more

However confident Tidey is, large amounts always reach a person. The single model gets the same guardrail.

Checks on demand

Jev picks the optional checks

Required checks always run. Jev decides whether the rest are worth running, which keeps each investigation short.

Write for people only

A summary only when someone will read it

Closed false alarms keep their evidence but skip the AI-written summary, which is most of the cost.

Tuned on one set, tested on another

Two sets of 50 synthetic alerts, each with an answer key neither version sees. Tidey was tuned on the first, then both versions ran once on the second.

The lesson

The question I didn’t ask.

Both misses passed every rule. Jev answered the question it was asked, and for both the customer was a fair answer. The fix is a new question, not a stricter number.

08Next Steps

What I’d do next

  1. 01
    Pattern rules

    A third question for Jev: “Is this risky even if the customer can explain it?”, for patterns like overnight cash at a non-bank ATM or a new device that failed two-factor. A high answer goes to a person.

  2. 02
    Re-test

    Run a fresh held-out set, so the new rules are checked on alerts they weren’t written for.

  3. 03
    Shadow mode

    Calibrate the lines on real labeled alerts, run next to analysts, and measure the minutes saved per alert.
